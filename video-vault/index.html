Same brief, different models

Clips made from the same written prompt by different video models, one try per model and shown as generated. They are not controlled tests: NMAI Research chose the shots, and the sizes and lengths differ as noted. The clips play muted.

Camel crossing. One prompt and one start frame, a frame from an earlier Caravan clip, given to Omni 1.1 Flash and Veo 3.1 Quality in Google Flow. Both clips are 1280 by 720 at 24 fps; Omni made 10 s and Veo 8 s.

The prompt

Side-on tracking shot at walking pace, camera level with the riders: a line of camel and rider silhouettes against a star-filled sky, lanterns swinging gently with each step. Context: the crest of a long dune. Night desert under a deep navy sky dense with stars, the Milky Way a soft pale band rising from lower left to upper right, no moon, no glow on the horizon. The only warm light comes from round cream paper lanterns with thin horizontal ribs and a small dark cap, always lit with a steady amber glow, hanging from the saddles. Single-humped camels walk forward at a steady pace, heads leading, all travelling from left to right across the frame. Riders wear dark indigo robes and head wraps and are seen only as silhouettes; no faces, no people on foot. Colours: indigo, navy and amber only. Smooth, slow cinematic camera, 16:9, full frame with no black bars, no vignette, no fisheye. A single continuous shot, no scene cuts, no text. Each camel keeps four legs that stay visible and move in a natural walking gait.

Omni made 10 s and Veo 8 s, both at 1280 by 720; Wan made 5 s at 768 by 448 with a fixed seed and a negative prompt listing things to avoid, people among them.

The three prompts

Dark headland. A lighthouse on a dark headland; its warm beam sweeps slowly across the storm clouds and the sea. Night, a heavy, uneasy world under too much weight. Palette: deep navy and near-black, with warm amber and gold light as the only warmth. No people walking; any person is a still silhouette in a window. No text, letters, numbers, logos, flags, weapons or real landmarks anywhere in the frame. Slow, steady cinematic camera, 16:9, full frame with no black bars, no vignette, no fisheye. A single continuous shot, no scene cuts.

Lamp and lens. The lighthouse lamp turns on: the great lens catches the light and begins to turn. Night, a heavy, uneasy world under too much weight. A single continuous shot, no scene cuts.

    Cost brief

    Approximate and dated: worked out from list prices and allowances read on 4 November 2026, which can change. An estimate at list price is not what a reader will pay.

    Spent, measured: about 1.91 billion tokens on Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code, counted from the session's transcripts, each message once: 17.4 million written out, 122.8 million written to the prompt cache, 1.77 billion read back from it, about 11,000 plain input. This is a floor: calls outside the transcripts may be missing, and the prep before 2 November and the song's own agent sessions aren't counted, so the estimate and the shares below may read low. Also 8.9 hours of local jobs, 5.8 of them on the GPU. The song: 90 Suno credits over 6 Creates and 12 takes, one take kept; the Create that made it cost 20.

    API estimate: about $1,323 at Anthropic's list price for Claude Opus 5.5: $20 a million written out, $5 or $8 a million written to the cache, $0.20 a million read back, $4 a million plain input. It rests on the transcript counts, not on Claude Code's own counter, as what that counter counts is unverified. The song: Suno has an API, but its site asks for a sign-in and shows no price, so there is no estimate.

    Share of a week, approximate: about 6 to 9 weeks of a Pro plan, about 1 to 2 weeks of Max, about 70 to 110% of a week of Ultra. The song: 90 of about 577 credits on Suno's Pro, 15.6%; 90 of about 2,308 on its Premier, 3.9%, a month's credits × 12 ÷ 52, a month's share divided down. Unused credits don't carry over, so this is an average.

    How the week is sized: Anthropic gives no token figure. It says Max is 5 times Pro's week and Ultra, the plan that was Max 20x, 10 times; Pro's own week isn't stated. So these rest on other people's estimates. Wokey, which sells a gateway to these models, data to 2 November 2026: a Pro week about $155 at list price, the middle half of accounts $146 to $173, or 24.2 million tokens that aren't cache reads, median of 79 accounts; the old Max 20x about $1,549, middle half $1,216 to $1,697, or 180 million, 18 accounts. FinOps LLM, which offers audits, updated 10 October 2026, from one user's logs on Opus 5: the old Max 20x about $1,860, Max 5x at least $1,217. The film against those: about $1,323, or about 140 million tokens that aren't cache reads. About 12% of those are written out, against about 6 to 7% on Wokey's accounts, and Wokey says an output token uses roughly 4 to 7 times the limit of an input token, so the readings by tokens may run low. What a week is worth also varies with the model, the mix of traffic and the account. Pro and Max also have a five-hour limit, and Anthropic says other limits may apply at its discretion, so a share isn't a promise of that much use.
